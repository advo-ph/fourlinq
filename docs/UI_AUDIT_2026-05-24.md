## The diagnosis

We have **143 brochure photos** in `/public/images/wp-export-originals/` and use almost none of them on the secondary pages. The body of FourlinQ marketing imagery exists; the build hasn't surfaced it.

Pages that should be **photo-led** (50%+ of viewport area is photography) are currently **text-led** (90%+ text with generic lucide icons).

Premium fenestration brands (Marvin, Sky-Frame, Vitrocsa, Schüco) all maintain the same discipline: every section gets a real photograph. We're maintaining the opposite — every section gets a lucide icon.

## Per-page findings + prescriptions

### 🚨 1. `/why-upvc` — wall of text (USER FLAGGED)

**State:** 6703px / 0 photos / 15 icons / 40 paragraphs / 1229 words

**Specific failures:**
- The "Built for how you actually live" section is **1799px tall** (27% of total page) with 7 icon+text cards. SaaS feature-list pattern.
- The comparison table is plain text in 4 columns. No visual treatment, no visual callouts (no checkmarks-vs-X, no graphical comparison).
- The "Three forces. One material" section is 838px of 3 icon cards. Same SaaS pattern repeated.
- Lucide icons (Eye, Flame, Sun, Shield, Clock, CloudRain, VolumeX) read as software-product icons, not premium architectural.
- Zero photography means the visitor never sees an actual uPVC profile in cross-section or in installation.

**The fix:**
- Replace 7 icon-cards with **7 photo-paragraph rows**, alternating L/R image vs body. We have brochure photos for every single benefit:
  - Attractive Appearance → `Walnut-Profile.jpg` (close-up of finish)
  - Fire Retardant → `Fire-Retardant.jpg`
  - Thermal Efficiency → `White-Profile.jpg` or a multi-chamber cross-section
  - Corrosion Resistant → `Corrosion-Resistance.jpg`
  - Long Lasting Performance → `Air-Water-Tight.jpg`
  - Weather Resistance → `Sound-Insulation.jpg` (paired with weather-resistance copy is fine)
  - Sound Insulation → `Sound-Insulation-1.jpg`
- Hardware close-up: `Stainless-Mechanism.jpg` for the "Two engineering details" paragraph in weather-resistance
- Replace the comparison table with a visual chart (or at least a checkmark/X column for each row)
- Drop lucide icons or relegate them to bullet markers

### 🚨 2. `/warranty` — all text, zero imagery

**State:** 5138px / 0 photos / 5 icons / 504 words

**Specific failures:**
- The "10" / "Year" hero is one of the strongest visual moments on the page. Then it descends into 4 walls of text.
- "What the warranty doesn't cover" section is just 4 paragraphs with em-dash bullets. No visual at all.
- The "3 steps" section uses numbered text. Could be photographed/illustrated.
- No images of FourlinQ at work — install crew, finished install, profile detail.

**The fix:**
- Add a hero photo behind the H1 title (or just below) showing a finished FourlinQ installation
- Per "what's covered" scope, add a small representative photo per scope (we have `Air-Water-Tight.jpg`, `Corrosion-Resistance.jpg`, `Sound-Insulation.jpg`)
- "Three steps" → add a quick install/factory/showroom photo per step
- Closing CTA already references the showroom — add a small showroom photo

### 🚨 3. `/brand` — icon overload (24 SVG, 1 photo)

**State:** 5983px / 1 photo / 24 icons / 291 words

**Specific failures:**
- 24 SVG icons across certifications, contact rows, showroom locator. Reads as a settings panel, not a brand story.
- Only 1 real photograph on the entire page. For a BRAND page.
- The showroom locator section uses generic map-pin icons rather than actual photos of each showroom
- Certifications use generic shield/award lucide icons rather than the real certification logos (or at least real photos of plaques on the wall)

**The fix:**
- Add a hero photograph of the FourlinQ workshop or a showroom interior at the top
- Each showroom card should have an actual photo (we have building exteriors in wp-export-originals: check `Company-Profile.jpg`, `Company_Profile1.jpg`, `FQC-Brand.jpg`, `FQC-Contact.jpg`)
- Certifications row — keep icons but make smaller, less prominent. Or use actual certification badge images if available.

### 🟠 4. `/finishes` — heavy text, only 1 image, swatches feel like color picker

**State:** 4372px / 1 photo / 9 icons / 291 words (in chrome)

**Specific failures:**
- The hero is the interactive finish-on-scene preview (good). But then everything below is text + swatch grid.
- The 11 pairing paragraphs we just rewrote are still text-only. They describe the finish but don't SHOW it on a real surface.
- Swatch grid is colored squares + faux-stripe overlay. We have actual finish profile photos available! (`Walnut-Profile.jpg`, `White-Profile.jpg`, `Golden-Oak-Profile.jpg`, `Black-Profile.jpg`, `Gray-Profile.jpg`, `Matte-Quartz-Profile.jpg`, `Skyframe-White_Profile.jpg`)

**The fix:**
- Swap the 11-swatch grid from `swatchHex` + stripe overlay to actual profile photos. Photos exist for at least 6 of 11.
- Add a 2-photo block per finish in the pairing section: real-world install showing the finish in context.

### 🟠 5. `/products/{windows,doors,specialist}` — decent photo density but all thumbnails

**State:** ~6000-7000px each / ~14 photos / ~10 icons

**What works:** cursor-switching gallery (4-5 photos per system, real PH context) + product card grid.

**What's missing:**
- No **profile cross-section diagram** showing why uPVC matters for that specific system type
- No **dimension callout** — for Slide & Fold we know "up to 6 m wide" but it's only in prose, not visual
- No **process shot** — measurement → fabrication → install
- The 6 advantages section is text-only (same pattern as why-upvc)

**The fix:**
- Add a single tasteful diagram block per system landing (profile cross-section + dimension callouts)
- The 6 advantages strip on each landing could become 6 photo-cards instead of icon-text cards

### 🟢 6. `/inspiration` + `/projects/:slug`

### 🟢 7. `/whats-new`

**State:** 3 entries, clean filter rail.

**What's missing:** more entries. (Tita-data-blocked.)

### 🟢 8. `/design-tool`, `/help-me-choose`, `/faq`, `/care`, `/for-architects`

These read as functional pages. Not the main premium-perception surfaces. Lower priority than the above.

## Closing observation

The site's visual problem isn't taste — type, color, spacing, and motion are all calibrated correctly. The problem is **we built a typography-first design system and forgot to surface the photography library we already own**. Marvin's secret isn't its serif font; it's that every section of every page is photographed. We can match that with assets we already have, no new shoots needed.
