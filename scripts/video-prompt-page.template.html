FourlinQ · product animation · 18 systems

Every system, prompted for video

One prompt per product in the Systems menu, ready to paste. Fourteen of them already ship as rendered tiles — those are the benchmark to beat, not a gap to fill. Four have no mechanism to render and need a generative model regardless.

What you're testing against. The fourteen baked tiles carry real mechanism: lever handles throwing before the leaf moves, cam locks turning a quarter, hinges, scissor stays that extend, actuator chains that lengthen link by link. They're deterministic and rebuild from one command. A generated clip has to hold all of that for 28 frames, and survive being played backwards.

Two conventions the bake settled, which any replacement has to match or the tiles won't sit together: the viewpoint is the interior face — that's where handles and locks are mounted, and where the 3D viewer's camera sits — and moving hardware is steel, not black, because black furniture on a black frame cannot be seen to move at tile size.

Before you paste

Each prompt is now one block — subject, motion, camera discipline, surface stability, strict panel counts and the prohibitions all merged. Grok Imagine has no separate negative field, so a negative left in its own box is a negative the tool never reads. Paste the whole thing. If your tool does have a negative field, every card has a collapsible section that splits it back apart.

  1. Click the thumbnails to copy the reference images. The actual bytes go to your clipboard — paste them straight in. For every baked system, 01.webp and 28.webp are the closed and open poses; attach both and it becomes an interpolation job rather than a generation job, which is far more reliable.
  2. Paste the full prompt. It runs long, and that length is doing work — the CAMERA, FRAMING, CONTINUITY and COMPLETION blocks are what stop the specific failures listed below.
  3. Watch it backwards. The tile scrubs in reverse on un-hover, so any settle, bounce or one-directional flourish looks broken half the time.

Every block below exists because of a real failure. Nothing here is precautionary — each was added after an output came back wrong.

THE PRODUCT IS ALONE — the model kept installing the window: a plaster reveal and recessed wall opening around it, screw heads at the corners, a floor visible through the glass. "Pure white seamless background" does not prevent this, because a white wall satisfies it. So the surround is now refused by name, and the glazing is told what colour it is rather than only what it is not.

FRAMING IS LOCKED — a Ken Burns push-in creeping across the clip; this pins the white margin explicitly, because "no zoom" alone did not hold. MOTION IS ONE CONTINUOUS GLIDE — the panel advancing in visible increments; now stated as a per-frame test rather than a list of things to avoid. THE MOVEMENT MUST FINISH — the slide stopping short of fully open, usually because the model spent the opening seconds holding on the closed pose and ran out of clip. CAMERA — three-quarter and slanted views. REALISM — dreamy, cinematic, soft-focus mood-film treatment. SURFACE — sparkle and ripple shimmer over the glass. STRICT COUNTS — duplicated leaves, openings from both sides at once, two panels sliding when only one should.

Each is attacked twice: once as a positive assertion the model can act on ("the empty white margin between the product and each of the four edges is the same in every frame"), then once as a prohibition. A geometric statement tends to survive where a style adjective like "locked-off" gets ignored.

These run long — around 9,000 characters. A silently truncated prompt loses its tail, which is where the prohibitions live. If your tool caps below that, every card has a Compact version at roughly 2,700 characters carrying the same constraints in condensed form.

Turning a finished clip into a tile

DUR=$(ffprobe -v error -show_entries format=duration -of csv=p=0 clip.mp4)
mkdir -p public/systems/anim/<id>
ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 \
  -vf "fps=28/${DUR},scale=640:360:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease,\
pad=640:360:(ow-iw)/2:(oh-ih)/2:white" \
  -frames:v 28 -q:v 80 "public/systems/anim/<id>/%02d.webp"
